Predicting Employee Churn

Python · Scikit-learn · View the full source code

Overview

The goal of this project was to identify employees who are likely to leave, using HR data on about 14,000 employees (satisfaction, tenure, salary, workload, complaints, evaluations and more). I aimed for an unbiased, end-to-end analysis: from cleaning and exploring the data to training and tuning a model.

This was one of my first projects, and much of the time went into machine learning. I chose not to build a dashboard here so that the focus stays on Python and ML.

Random Forest clearly outperformed the other models, catching 92% of the employees who left with 95% precision and 97% overall accuracy. I lowered the decision threshold from 0.5 to 0.4 so the model leans toward flagging at-risk employees, since missing someone who is about to leave costs HR more than a false alarm. A simpler version using only the five most correlated features still reached an F1-score of 0.82.

Recommendations

The heatmap points to four key factors HR should address:

  • Satisfaction: Run regular, anonymous surveys to spot dissatisfaction trends early and encourage honest feedback.
  • Career development: Offer training programs, mentoring and a clear career path for employees who want to grow.
  • Salary: Introduce performance-based pay such as bonuses, commissions or stock options for employees who exceed expectations.
  • Filed complaints:
    • Respond promptly: this is the most effective lever. Employees should feel heard and see real action on their issues.
    • Analyze the nature of complaints: group them into themes (team conflict, workload, harassment…) to understand root causes.
