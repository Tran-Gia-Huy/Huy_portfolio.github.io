US Retail Sales

2014 – 2017

Question: Where is a US retailer of furniture, office supplies and technology losing money, and which customers should it focus on?

Approach: Cleaned 9,994 order lines (2014–2017) in Excel, then built an interactive Power BI dashboard with a profit decomposition tree and RFM customer segmentation.

Result: Found that "Tables" lost $17.7K while profit overall nearly doubled, pinpointed the five states behind those losses, and identified a high-value "At-Risk" segment worth about $90K in profit.

Overview

I chose this project because I wanted to see how data can uncover insights and drive business decisions.

Working with real sales and profit data let me apply my analytical skills in a practical context. It was also a chance to build a dashboard that is both attractive and easy to use, which sharpened my Power BI skills.

Key Insights

1. The "Tables" sub-category keeps losing money

While total profit almost doubled from $49.5K in 2014 to $93.4K in 2017, the "Tables" sub-category lost money every single year: about $3K a year from 2014 to 2016, then $8.1K in 2017, close to half of its $17.7K four-year loss. Sales of tables were flat at about $61K in both 2016 and 2017, so the bigger loss came from lower margins, not lower demand. Overall, the business loses 8.6 cents on every dollar of tables it sells.

The losses are concentrated in a handful of states. Tables were profitable in 18 states, but these five lost more than the whole sub-category's net loss:

2. Customer segmentation: high-value customers are at risk

Customer accounts were scored on Recency (how recently they bought), Frequency (how often) and Monetary value (how much they spent), then grouped into 11 standard RFM segments.

The business currently lacks New and Promising customers, a gap that could cause problems in the future.

Potential Loyalists are the largest group (about 1,100 customer accounts) but generate only about $12K in profit. This is acceptable for now: they are recent buyers with modest spending, and the goal is to grow them into loyal customers.

The priority should be the At-Risk customers: a similar number of accounts (about 1,100), but generating about $90K in profit. They spend a lot but have not purchased recently. Targeted campaigns could win them back, for example:

  1. Loyalty program: reward points on every purchase that can be redeemed for discounts.
  2. Exclusive offers: personalized promotions based on each customer's purchase history.

To support this, I prepared a customer segment list with each customer's most recent purchases, which a sales team could use to understand their interests.
